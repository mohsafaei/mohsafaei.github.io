🧬 **Our book chapter has been published**

Our chapter, **“Toward Responsive Polymer Actuators Based on Novel Composite Structures,”** has been published in *Smart Polymers for Sensing Technologies* (Springer Series in Materials Science, Vol. 360, 2026).

We analyzed more than 20,000 Scopus-indexed articles published between 2020 and 2024 to identify emerging research directions in the field. One notable trend is the rapid growth of the literature: the annual number of publications on hydrogels increased from approximately 700 to 1,400 during this period.

The chapter reviews three major categories of stimuli-responsive polymer actuators:

⚡ **Electroactive polymers** — including dielectric elastomers capable of strains of up to 314%, ionic hydrogels with reported stretchability of up to 470% and conductivity of up to 3,100 S/cm, and ionic polymer–metal composites (IPMCs) demonstrating stability over more than 250,000 fatigue cycles.

🌡 **Thermo-responsive polymers** — including 4D-printed PLA–TPU shape-memory polymers with shape fixity of up to 100%; LCST hydrogels, such as Al-alginate/PNIPAM interpenetrating polymer networks, with reported compressive strength of 6.3 MPa and stretchability of 9.95×; and TPMS-structured composites designed to improve the uniformity of Joule heating.

🧲 **Magneto-responsive polymers** — including Fe₃O₄-filled composites for remote actuation, untethered star-shaped hydrogel microgrippers for handling cancer cells, and dual-trigger magnetic shape-memory polymers with reported shape recovery of up to 70%.

A key takeaway is the growing importance of coupled electro-chemo-thermo-mechanical models for the predictive design of responsive polymer composites.
